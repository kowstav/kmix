# Current answers: coverage, descendants, compute and masking

6 October 2026, after the wider native codec and partial mask pilot completed.

## What has and has not been thoroughly tested

**Thorough testing of every one of the 96 current ideas is incomplete.** The
historical round 1–7 audit recovered 95 families; the new rarity-mask proposal
adds A96 to the current ledger. That does not prove every historical idea was
recovered. All 96 remain open. The audit distinguishes historical proxies from
the architectures actually proposed. Some early tests were narrower than the
proposal and did not justify dismissing its potential.

118 finite native/cache heads have completed ideal-objective optimization
certificates, and 27 online role policies have been evaluated. The 36 saved
mask-training rungs are additional experiments, not 36 ideas or additional
certified architectural optima. Many variants and combinations remain unbuilt:
full learned retention/rejection, ordered constructor infill, nonlinear relation
gating, original-transformer masking and matched-capacity visual mechanisms.

The current rigorous work includes normalized probability laws, explicit
decoder information contracts, exact integer predictions, mathematical gradient
and curvature checks, finite-box optimality gaps, prefix causality, independent
decoding, immutable source hashes, and code/model/resource accounting. These
establish particular implementations and bounded model-family optima. They
cannot establish absolute potential, globally best descendants or effort
equivalent to the collective development of attention and LLMs for every idea.
I have not completed that broader research program or tested every combination.

## A stronger descendant that actually changed the result

The earlier source residual's coefficient was on its upper bound while the
loss derivative still pointed toward a larger value. A registered wider box
removed that restriction. Its ideal finite-box optimum gap is 0.000170 byte;
its priced A/B development gain is 81.728 bytes. Frozen coefficients then
improved fresh E/F Q16 likelihoods by 109.927 and 43.911 bytes.

The **actual native archives** now save **110 and 44 bytes**, totaling **154
bytes on 1 MiB**. Both independently decode exactly. Every candidate integer
prediction equals the numerical replay, and core hidden states are unchanged.
The lab executable grew by 784 bytes, so the sampled payload gain does not
establish a net package gain. Full warm archive, production packing and whole
CPU/RAM qualification remain unmeasured. No 20% result is claimed.

This gives an evidence-based next descendant: adapt source utility causally
using a finite probability mixture or a compact Fisher-preconditioned update.
The pending protocol is research_round8_source_adaptive_descendant_protocol.md.
It supplies explicit updates, a finite-expert ideal regret guarantee, Q16 tax
and native decoder gates. It is a proposed experiment, not an executed success.

## Required 5090 hours

**Paid 5090 time needed now for current small residuals and CPU codec pilots:
zero.** The newly completed ten-policy, two-seed mask pilot took 56.79 seconds
on a free T4, including loading/evaluation. This says nothing about throughput
for the original transformer or larger new architectures.

For a demanding shortlist, the provisional allocation is at most four billed
hours for readiness/benchmarking, then 12–24 GPU-hours for screening and 8–16
for two finalists, conditional on measured throughput, native export readiness
and the existing $70 caps. This is not an exhaustive all-96 estimate.

For example, 18 arms at 128 steps, six extended to 384 and three to 1152 cost
6144 steps per seed. Two seeds at 2^20 useful targets per step imply
12,884,901,888 target exposures. At a hypothetical 250,000 useful targets per
GPU-second, that is 14.32 GPU-hours before setup/evaluation. Real batch sizes,
mask exposure, precision, data movement and architecture must be benchmarked.
Two GPUs for one hour are two GPU-hours. Dollar limits, not an assumed card
speed or current price, determine the schedule. Spending so far is $0.

Short runs are appropriate for screening and catching bugs. They do not prove
that a slow-learning architecture cannot work. Preserve uncertain curves and
compare useful-target exposure, capacity and complete native loss. The detailed
budget, equations and continuation ladder are in
research_round8_5090_hours_and_rarity_plan.md and the attachment training addendum.

## Whether rare/hard words impair common-word prediction

The hypothesis is plausible. A complete lexical census now records 127,049,170
tokens and 1,208,329 distinct types under its explicit ASCII/casefold convention.
Corpus-wide singletons are 0.482459% of occurrences; words appearing once within
their article account for 24.788358%. These are very different masking policies.
The gzip TSV preserves every word's corpus count/fraction, article coverage and
maximum/mean within-article fraction. It excludes XML metadata and some spellings;
these counts are not native bit-cost measurements.

The free-T4 pilot retained all inputs and masked or downweighted complete native
dictionary-code target spans at three thresholds. It reached 128/384/1152 steps
with two seeds and an unweighted control. The residual's initial common/rare
gradient cosine was -0.683 on one fixed batch, demonstrating local conflicting
directions in that small head. **Original transformer interference is unmeasured.**

On fit-held-out C/D, masking gave no consistent total advantage over unweighted
training. Half-weighting at the mild cutoff was 0.327 byte worse on the two-seed
mean; zero-weighting was 1.690 bytes worse. Its incremental improvement over the
wider residual was roughly 9–12 bytes against 1288 parameter bytes for two
copies. No candidate archive or net package gain follows. The report explains
OOV/metadata limitations, exposure confounds and two differing GPU/reference
CDF events; all model and optimizer checkpoints are preserved.

The stronger follow-up keeps rare context, aligns literal/OOV and metadata spans,
tests class gradients across multiple blocks/layers, and compares soft weighting,
gradient projection and common/rare specialist heads. Every outcome remains
reachable and all-target loss is the decision metric. Any class/gating law must
normalize and be derived from decoded history; unknown future-word labels cannot
choose an expert for free. Finish any mask curriculum with ordinary all-target
calibration. See research_round8_rarity_residual_pilot_report.md for precise
equations, implementation limits and continuation steps.
